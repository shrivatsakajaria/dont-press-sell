# Don't Press Sell: product context

This file is the product brief the impeccable skill asks for. It is written from the planning conversation with the owner, SK, in place of the skill's own interview. The full detail is in the design document.

## What it is
A free, one-button, one-minute phone web game. The player holds or sells $10,000 of play money through a real historical fall in the US stock market, without knowing whether it is a real crash or a false alarm, and ends with a newspaper front page about how they did.

## Who it is for
People who meet it in a social feed or a group chat, on a phone, usually inside an in-app browser: retail traders and finance people, and also people with no finance background at all. Nobody has asked for it; it has about five seconds to earn the next five.

## What it must make them feel
The pull of a bet. A stomach drop when the page turns red, the urge to press, regret in both directions, and the wish for one more go. Then something funny enough to post.

## Mode
Experience. The visitor is inside the work from the first frame. There is no landing page, no menu and no sign-up.

## Character
Dry, deadpan, exact. A newspaper that takes the market seriously and the player not at all.

## World
A financial newspaper: pink newsprint, black ink, red ink, rules instead of boxes, a masthead, a folio line, headlines.

## The one rich thing
The paper itself. It is calm and pink, deepens as the player loses, floods red on the worst days and drains to grey when the player is out of the market. Everything else stays quiet.

## Signature detail
The front page about you: "Local investor sells three days before the bottom."

## Anti-references
A trading dashboard. A casino or betting app. A dark page with an orange accent. Anything that looks made by default: purple gradients, glow, glass, cards in a grid, emoji, Inter or Fraunces.

## Voice
News lines are straight and factual. Commentary is funny in plain English. Verdict titles are trading slang, each with a plain line after it. British spelling. No exclamation marks, no emoji.

## Hard limits
The name "S&P 500" never appears; the market is "the US stock market". Dollars. Every figure and date is correct. Days that report deaths or disasters carry no jokes. No personal data, no accounts, no ads, no cost.
